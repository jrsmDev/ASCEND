About ASCEND

I have spent seven years in the fitness industry, competed twice, and coached people at every level: first-time lifters, busy parents, athletes, and clients coming back from injury.

ASCEND started a couple of months ago, not as a product but as the training log I wanted for myself. I was tired of apps that were loud between sets, expensive to keep, or eager to hold the one thing that should belong to the person doing the work: the record.

So I built the smallest useful thing first. A place to put the plan, the sets, the numbers, and the story they tell over time. Then I kept using it. Every awkward tap at the rack became the next fix. The guided workout flow, the weight pre-fills, the rest timer, the body-weight check-in: none of that came from a feature list. It came from standing in the gym with the thing in my hand.

ASCEND has grown into a complete training system: plans, progression, muscle maps, body weight, 19 languages, importers for FitNotes, Strong, Hevy and Apple Health, and a native Android app. It is free, open source under the AGPL-3.0, and it runs on a phone or on a server you control.

I am Joseph Rman Madrilejos, jrsmDev. I design and build ASCEND, and I still log every session with it. That is the quality bar: I am the first user of every release. If you train with it, I want to hear what is missing.

Principles

  • Your data is yours. Local-first, one-file backups, no lock-in. Leaving ASCEND must always be one export away.
  • No telemetry, ever. If a feature needs analytics to justify itself, it does not ship.
  • Dependency-light. The backend is plain Node with two dependencies; the frontend is React, a router and a store. Small enough to actually audit.
  • Open source that stays open. AGPL-3.0, so every fork and every hosted version owes its code back to everyone.
